What this exercise is & how to rate

Objectives: identify your most valuable skills, identify your potential skills, and gain insight into how to leverage the first and develop the second.

Go through the list and rate every skill twice:

Skill level — 1 = no skill, 5 = expert Enjoyment — 1 = not at all, 5 = I really love using this skill

Once both are set you'll see a skill coefficient: skill level × enjoyment (maximum 25). It helps you spot your most valuable skills, the ones you are good at and enjoy.

Skills where enjoyment is 4 or 5 but skill level is 3 or lower are your potential skills. With focused development they could become real strengths. If a skill is missing from the list, add it at the end of section 1.

Used to label the report and the saved PDF filename. Everything stays on this device.

Missing a skill?

2 · Your skills profile

Averages by category, highest score first. Only skills you have rated count toward each average.

3 · My most valuable skills

List up to 10 of your highest-scoring skills, the ones you are strong at and truly enjoy. Order them by importance or preference with the arrows. For each, rate how much you use it in your current work and note how you could use it more.

Questions to reflect on
  • How much do these skills contribute to your sense of purpose and motivation at work?
  • Are there current projects or tasks where you could apply them more often?
  • What small but practical opportunities exist to bring them into use? Even small steps can make your work more engaging.
  • If your current role offers limited scope, which other roles or career paths would let you use them more often, and more meaningfully?

4 · My potential skills

List up to 10 skills you enjoy (4 or 5) but rate 3 or lower for skill level. Pick the ones where you already have some ability. For each, note where you use it now, inside or outside work, and what it would take to develop it.

Ideas for developing a skill
  • Start applying it somewhere small and low-risk, at work or in a personal project, hobby or community activity.
  • Learn from someone who is strong at it.
  • Take a short course, read, or practise deliberately on a regular schedule.
  • Ask for feedback and use it to adjust.
